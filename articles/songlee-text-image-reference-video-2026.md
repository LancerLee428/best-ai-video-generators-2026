# Text, Image or References? A Smarter Start to AI Video with SongLee

*By LancerLee428, operator of SongLee. Creative workflow guide based on public product information checked October 2, 2026; the example briefs below are suggestions, not generated test results.*

One of the most appealing things about [SongLee](https://seedancevideo.online/) is that it lets the starting material guide the video workflow. A sentence, an image and a group of references each call for a different kind of direction. Having those model-dependent options in one workspace makes the creative decision easier to see.

This is a terrific setup for moving from an idea to a specific generation request. Begin with what you have, choose the input path supported by your model and plan, and review the credit estimate before you submit.

## Start with text when the scene lives in your imagination

Text-to-video is a useful choice when you are inventing a setting or a moment from scratch. Give the prompt a subject, a visible action, a camera direction and an atmosphere.

For example: “A paper boat moves through a shallow stream at sunrise. The camera follows beside it at water level. Warm light, gentle movement, a quiet scene.” This is a suggested creative brief, not evidence of a SongLee output.

That structure helps you make deliberate choices. You can change the action or camera direction and keep the rest of the brief stable as you explore another version. [Open text-to-video](https://www.seedancevideo.online/video?mode=text-to-video) and confirm that the chosen model supports the settings you need.

## Start with an image when you have a visual anchor

Image-to-video is an appealing route for a prepared opening frame, a permitted product image or an original character illustration. Use the image to establish the visual starting point, then describe the movement you want to explore.

For a product concept, a brief might request a slow camera move around the object and a subtle change in lighting. Begin with a simple direction, review the resulting details and decide whether a later attempt needs another motion or angle.

The [image-to-video workspace](https://www.seedancevideo.online/video?mode=image-to-video) shows the controls supported by the selected model. Image support and reference limits vary, and a supplied image does not guarantee perfect product or character preservation.

## Use references when the brief has several anchors

Some supported model and plan combinations offer multi-reference inputs. This route is worth exploring when a project needs several permitted visual or audiovisual references, rather than only a starting sentence or frame.

Decide what each reference is intended to communicate: a subject, a setting, a movement direction or another supported aspect of the brief. Use the [multi-reference entry](https://www.seedancevideo.online/video?mode=multi-reference) to check the actual media types and limits available to your account.

The value here is creative flexibility. You can select a workflow with the inputs appropriate for the project, while keeping your parameters and estimated credits visible in the studio. Availability depends on the model and plan; this guide does not suggest that all three paths are enabled for every combination.

## Make your first experiment easy to evaluate

Choose one brief and a clear review question. Decide the aspect ratio for the intended destination, select an available duration and resolution, and note the quoted credits. After generation, review the entire clip before changing one main variable for the next attempt.

SongLee's [pricing page](https://www.seedancevideo.online/pricing) explains the subscription balance and expiry rules. Use that together with the current generation quote when planning experiments.
